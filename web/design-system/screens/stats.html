2026-09-24 — the receive ring, counted

A Network card: the Ethernet MAC's packet and loss totals. Two are new — Rx ring overruns, frames the MAC discarded because no receive descriptor was free, and Rx fifo overflows, frames lost before the ring. The MAC keeps both in a read-to-clear register nothing read, so until now a ring too small for its traffic lost frames with every counter at zero. They are plain rows, not faults: the ring is sized against them, and TCP resends what they lose.

2026-09-21 — several gear answered, and the row that says so

The DALI card gains the five rows of our own backward window, by cause. Four of them existed on /api/v1/stats since ISSUE-104 and were drawn nowhere; the fifth is new: Backward multi answer — a §8.2.5 violation behind a broadcast, group or COMPARE frame, which is several real gear answering one window and failing to align. It is the bus-health probe's ordinary reading (2 of 8 probes on the bench that day), so it is a plain row and never warn; the three rows above it keep meaning "one gear was asked and its answer was unreadable" and are gated at zero. Late rejected stays the control.

2026-09-16 — a cached number said so, and four that reached no gate

Internal largest block carries @boot, on the row and in the tile foot (ISSUE-90). The walk behind it — every block of every matching heap under the allocator's critical section — masks the PHY interrupt for 1,7-4,5 ms, so it moved to the boot stages and the live read returns the cache. That was the right move and it left a stale number sitting between two live ones, on a five-second poll, in a card with delta chips. The suffix is the whole fix: a stale number is a defect only while it is served as a current one.

The DALI card gains four rows from the interrupt's own accounting (ISSUE-89): ticks it found missing, ticks serviced twice, ticks the interrupt itself timed as late, and the worst gap. Until now they existed only in a serial line, which is to say the acceptance of that remainder rested on a human reading a log. Two are gated at zero on the bench; late is not, because 55 of them against zero corrupted captures is a healthy board — the decoder tolerates one lost sample per tick.

And the three blocks this card announced on 2026-09-01 and never drew — Home Assistant bridge, Input devices, Rules engine — are drawn. The screen has had them since that day; the card's changelog said "Added" above markup that showed nothing. Exactly the self-concealing shape the entry below describes, one level up.

2026-09-05 — the bus load tile

A fourth tile: DALI bus load, the device's own one-second occupancy gauge — the same number the dashboard card and the OLED strip show, because it is computed once on the device and never in a browser. Its foot carries our own share and the sniffer's foreign forward-frame count, which together answer "busy — with whom?". The DALI card gains the three rows behind the tile; the two per-mille rows are gauges and show no change column.

2026-09-01 — three blocks that reached no screen

/api/v1/stats publishes eight blocks and this page rendered five. The Home Assistant bridge, every Part 103 input figure and the entire rules engine — nineteen counters — were computed, serialized, sent, and displayed nowhere. Added as Home Assistant bridge, Input devices and Rules engine.

The defect is self-concealing, which is why it lasted: every other block IS on the page, so it looks complete, and there is no count to check it against. scripts/verify_read_surface.py is that count now — it compares the DTO's blocks against the screen's and fails on the next one, with an allowlist whose entry count only ever goes down.

mqtt.connected is a boolean and renders as a chip in the card header rather than being coerced into the counter grid: a connection state has no delta, and a "0" beside two free-running totals invites reading it as a count. The rules block's live figures — timers_active, rules_loaded, vars_in_use and the three latency percentiles — are declared gauges for the same reason: a falling timers_active is a timer that fired, not a wrapped u32.
